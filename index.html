About This Project
⚠️ Illustrative Data

All metrics displayed are simulated data created for educational and visualization purposes. They are designed to reflect plausible real-world patterns documented in public-health literature, but they are not sourced from official measurements. Do not use this data for policy or legal decisions.

What This Map Shows

  • Voter Turnout — % of registered voters participating in local elections
  • Pro-Health Votes — share of votes for candidates who championed clean water, lead abatement, and public-health investment
  • Lead Contamination — estimated average lead in tap water (ppb). EPA action level is 15 ppb
  • School Performance — composite index of test scores and graduation rates (0–100)

The Story in the Data

Public-health research consistently finds that lead exposure — even at low levels — impairs cognitive development in children, depresses educational attainment, and can suppress long-term civic participation. Communities with aging infrastructure (older pipes, buildings built before 1978) face disproportionate exposure.

The scatter plot in this tool shows a strong negative correlation between lead levels and school scores across NYC community districts — a pattern supported by peer-reviewed epidemiological studies.

Real Data Sources to Explore

  • NYC DOHMH Lead Poisoning Prevention Program
  • NYC Open Data — Board of Elections turnout files
  • NYC Department of Education — School Quality Reports
  • EPA Safe Drinking Water Information System
  • NYC Community Health Profiles

How to Use

Toggle between the four data layers using the buttons in the header. Click any district on the map to see its full profile, a radar chart comparing across all dimensions, and its position in the city-wide lead-vs-schools scatter plot.
